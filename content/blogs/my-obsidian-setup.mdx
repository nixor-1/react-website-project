# MOC structure

My Obsidian vault makes use of the so-called MOC structure, a concept popularized by Nick Milo and/or invented by him in the world of personal knowledge management (PKM) and Obsidian. Implementing an MOC structure involves structuring certain files (in the case of Obsidian, `.md` files) as hubs, linking to other files which share that hub's theme or subject. These files that are linked to may in turn be either another MOC file, or a regular note which does not inherently act as a hub. The concept is thus quite simple to understand, but it may not be as simple to understand why it is needed, at least not to the note-taking novice 🧐.

## Initial setup and its limitations

Initially, after installing Obsidian, I simply created folders for every theme and subject in which I wanted to sort other folders and notes. However, after some time doing this, it became quite clear that such a setup had its limitations, with the premier being that notes could not reside in two folders at once. This limitation only kept becoming more clear with an ever-increasing number of notes in my Obsidian vault.

If you are technically inclined, you may wonder if I could not have solved this limitation with symlinks, symbolically linking a file to all the folder locations in which I wanted it to be located. There are two reasons for this:

# Filenames

I have decided that all filenames in my vault follow the kebab-case naming scheme, changing all non-standard ASCII characters and symbols to their standard ASCII equivalents. For example, the Danish letters 'æ', 'ø', and 'å' are respectively converted to 'ae', 'oe', and 'aa'. This was simply because I prefer this naming scheme and it makes them more manageable in a world dominated by English letters. It has happened more than once that issues arose with my own last name (Jørgensen) in software.

# Scripts

I have implemented and employed several Python scripts to keep my Obsidian vault in the shape and form that I desire. These can be found here: https://github.com/nixor-1/obsidian-scripts. For example, the script `rename-filenames.py` implements the kebab-case naming scheme.

I will not go over them all, as brief descriptions of them can be found through the above link.

# Note writing and structure

I have a somewhat unorthodox way of writing and structuring my notes, where every note may at surface level seem to sometimes consist of disparate notes. I have chosen to embed all notes in either of two callbacks: ones with titles (by far most of them), and ones without. These are styled with CSS to clearly show a thin square around the text, with the title just above, so that one may immediately and clearly distinguish between individual text/note blocks in a note file.
